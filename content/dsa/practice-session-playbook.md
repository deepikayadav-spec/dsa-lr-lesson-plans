# Practice Session Playbook

**For instructors, any practice block, any duration.** Same ritual every time — only the clock changes. Read once fully; after that, use the **cheat sheet** at the end.

Text in quotes is what you say out loud, close to verbatim.

---

## 1. What this is

A structured, timed work session — not a lecture, not silent seatwork.

- Students attempt problems themselves, alone first, then with their pod.
- You check progress out loud every 10–15 minutes.
- You solve, at the end, only the problem the room actually got stuck on.
- Every student shows one passing output before leaving.

**The one rule that matters most:** run the periodic check every time — even when the room looks busy — and never let anyone leave without showing you a result.

---

## 2. Before students arrive (5 min)

1. Practice URL up on the projector — the actual problem page, not a menu.
2. Progress tracker drawn on the board (one column per pod, one row per problem).
3. Countdown timer visible, running for the whole block.
4. Red slips (or sticky notes) at the end of each row — the "I'm stuck" signal, see §5.
5. Read the problem set yourself beforehand. Know which one is hardest — that's usually the one you'll live-solve.
6. Decide the block length before you walk in (§4 shows how the script scales) and write checkpoint minutes on the board.

---

## 3. Pods and anchors (set up once per term)

**Groups of 7–8 seats.** A pod is a block of seats, not a chosen group — if you sit there, you're in that pod. Nobody picks, nobody moves.

Pods needed ≈ class size ÷ 7.5, rounded so no pod exceeds 8:

| Class size | Pods |
|---|---|
| 50 | 7 |
| 70 | 9 |
| 85 | 11 |
| 100 | 13 |

**Setup, once:** sketch pod boundaries on the seating chart, tape a letter at each block's aisle end, announce it once ("the letter nearest you is your pod, all term"), ask each pod for a volunteer anchor (if none, the aisle seat is the anchor by default), and match board columns to pod order left-to-right.

**The anchor's only 3 jobs:** mark ticks when a pod member finishes; escalate a red slip to you if the pod's stuck 2+ minutes; confirm every pod member has a passing output before they leave. Tell them once: *"You're not a tutor. You count, and you escalate."*

**When things shift:** absentee → pod's just smaller, don't re-pod. Anchor absent → aisle seat covers. Wrong seat → that's their pod today. New joiner → wherever they sit.

---

## 4. Scaling to any duration

The reference script (§7–§11) is written for **60 minutes**. Six pieces are fixed length no matter the total — launch, the together-problem, each check, and the live solve don't shrink. What flexes is **silent work time**, split into two stretches (Route A, then Route B).

**Fixed pieces (33 min, always):** Launch 5 · Problem 1 together 7 · Norm-setting + Check 1, 3 · Check 2, 3 · Check 3, 3 · Live solve 7 · Exit gate 5.

**Flexible piece:** `Work time = Total − 33 min`, split ~55% Route A / 45% Route B.

| Total block | Work time | Route A | Route B |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 min | 12 min | 7 | 5 |
| 60 min | 27 min | 15 | 12 |
| 75 min | 42 min | 23 | 19 |
| 90 min | 57 min | 31 | 26 |

**Under ~35 min:** drop to 2 checks, cut Problem-1-together to 5 min (or skip if this is a same-day repeat block). Never cut the live solve or exit gate.

Write your block's real clock times on the board before students arrive — don't do the subtraction live.

---

## 5. The red-slip signal

Stuck more than **2 minutes** → put a red slip on the screen, visibly, no raised hand. You or the anchor come over. This exists so students don't sit stuck in silence, and so you don't have to scan every screen to find who needs help. Struggle under 2 minutes is left alone on purpose.

State it once, every block: *"Stuck for more than two minutes — not ten — put a red slip on your screen. Don't raise your hand, don't turn around."*

---

## 6. The periodic check ("the ladder")

Run it at roughly **25%, 55%, 85%** through the block (60-min script: minutes 15, 30, 45). Takes 90 seconds — it's the single biggest thing separating this from a room where you can't tell who's stuck.

> *"Everyone stop. Hands up if you've finished at least one."* — count out loud —
> *"Keep them up if you've done two."* — count — *"Three."* — count —

Write the counts in the corner of the board as you go, then:

- **Compare to the last check, out loud:** *"Two-or-more went from forty-four to sixty-one. Keep going."*
- **Name the sticking point you saw circulating** — the problem, not a student.

**Never name a student who hasn't started.** **Never skip a check because the room looks busy** — the check is what makes it look busy. If a number goes flat between checks, change something *now* — walk faster, drop a hint to the whole room, shorten the next stretch.

---

## 7. Launch (fixed, ~5 min)

Stand at the front. Wait for quiet.

> *"Laptops open. Go to the URL on the screen — right now. I'm looking at the room in thirty seconds and I want the same page on every screen."*

Wait 30 s. Scan the room, say what you see: *"Row three, I'll wait."* Then state the deal, filling in your real minute numbers:

> *"Here's the hour. In [X] minutes everyone finishes problem one — I'll walk you into it. Then you work. At [Y] minutes I solve whatever most of you got stuck on. Before you leave, you show me or your anchor one problem that runs. That's the deal."*

---

## 8. Problem 1 together (fixed, ~7 min)

1. **Read it aloud**, the whole statement.
2. **Ask for the approach, not code:** *"In plain English, what does this program have to do?"* Take two answers.
3. **Everyone types it themselves** — say the structure out loud, don't type it on the projector.
4. **Countdown, then run:** *"Three, two, one — hit run."*
5. **Hands up who got it.** Count out loud. Anchors mark it.

If a student's code doesn't run — don't fix it. Say: *"Read the error and tell me the line number."*

---

## 9. Norm-setting, work stretches, and checks

**Norm-setting (fixed, ~3 min):** state the red-slip rule (§5) and that you'll check progress every so often — *"hands, not names, get used to it."* Then run **Check 1**.

**Work stretches:** silent solo work, timer visible, you circulate. Split the room in two so you personally reach every seat once:

- **Route A** = the half you'd normally reach last (back/corners). Runs first.
- **Route B** = the other half. Runs second — pod talk is allowed here.

**What to say at each desk:**

| What you see | Say this |
|---|---|
| Problem open, nothing typed | *"Read me the first line you're going to write."* |
| Code typed, not run | *"Run it. Let's see what it says."* |
| Error on screen | *"Read the error out loud. What line?"* |
| Looks finished | *"Show me your output."* |
| Off the platform entirely | *"Which problem are you on?"* — flat, no scolding |

Never touch a student's keyboard. 90 seconds max per desk. Note what people are stuck on — you need it for the live solve.

**Route B's pod talk:** *"You may talk to your pod now, quietly — explaining it is how you find out if you actually understand it."* Priority: red slips first, then screens that haven't changed since Route A.

**Stuck and frustrated?** Hand them to a peer, don't rescue them yourself: *"Priya finished this two minutes ago — walk him through your approach, don't give him the code."*

---

## 10. Live solve (fixed, ~7 min)

Solve the problem you actually saw people stuck on — not one picked in advance. If you didn't circulate, you're guessing here.

- Type it live, from scratch, talking through your thinking.
- Make a deliberate mistake and read the error aloud — errors are normal, not a sign of failure.
- Pull the next line from the room: *"What goes on line two?"*

> *"If you attempted this already, this is landing for you. If not, this is what you'll redo at home — and it'll take three times as long alone."*

---

## 11. Exit gate (fixed, ~5 min)

> *"Before you leave: one problem that runs, shown to me or your anchor. Anchors — confirm your pod."*

Stand at the door, visible, not blocking. Then: *"In chat or to your pod, say which problem you're doing first next time."*

Read the board on the way out, citing the real trend: *"Fifty-two of you finished two or more today, up from eleven three weeks ago."*

---

## 12. When something goes off-script

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| Projector/platform down at launch | Announce the delay, give students problem 1 to discuss on paper while you fix it — never leave the room idle. |
| Attendance far below normal | Run pods smaller, don't merge. Under ~20 total, skip pods and just do straight show-of-hands checks. |
| Room finishing early | Pull in a bonus problem or preview next session's problem 1 — say that's what you're doing. Don't stretch the live solve. |
| Room behind (numbers flat at Check 2) | Change something immediately: shorten Route B, drop a hint to the whole room, or move the live solve earlier. |
| One pod struggling as a group | Spend a full circulate pass there in Route B — that's an anchor-escalation pattern, not individual red slips. |
| Hybrid/remote students | Ladder counts need a poll/reaction channel. Red slips become a chat keyword ("STUCK"). |
| First time running this yourself | Run it exactly as written for 2 blocks before adapting — the ritual is the point. |

---

## 13. Cheat sheet (60-min reference — scale per §4)

| Time | Block | You are doing |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Launch | Scripted start, scan screens, state the deal |
| 5–12 | Problem 1 together | Read aloud, take approach, countdown, count hands |
| 12–15 | Norm-setting + Check 1 | Red-slip rule → ladder count |
| 15–30 | Work stretch 1 (Route A) | Back/corners first, scripted questions, 90 s cap |
| 30–33 | Check 2 | Ladder count, compare, name the sticking point |
| 33–45 | Work stretch 2 (Route B) | Pod talk allowed, red slips first |
| 45–48 | Check 3 | Ladder count, read the trend |
| 48–55 | Live solve | Type live, deliberate mistake, pull answers from the room |
| 55–60 | Exit gate | Passing output each, next-session commitment |

**Three checks, every block, no exceptions.** Roughly 25% · 55% · 85% through, whatever the length.
