# Practice Session Playbook — Instructor Instruction Book

**For instructors. Any practice block, any duration.** Same ritual every time — only the clock changes. Read this once fully before your first block; after that, use the **One-Page Strip** at the end as your running cheat sheet.

Text in quotes is what you say out loud, verbatim or close to it.

---

## 0. What this session is and isn't

A practice block is **not** a lecture, **not** a doubt-clearing hour, and **not** silent seatwork you supervise from the front. It is a structured, timed work session where:

- Every student attempts problems themselves, alone first, then with their pod.
- You measure progress out loud every 10–15 minutes so the room feels the clock.
- You solve, at the end, only the problem the room actually got stuck on — never one you picked in advance.
- Every student shows one passing output before leaving.

If you take nothing else from this book: **run the periodic check every time, don't skip it because the room looks busy, and never let anyone leave without showing you a result.**

---

## 1. Before students arrive (5 min)

1. **Projector** — the practice URL on screen, large. The actual problem page, not a menu or dashboard.
2. **Board** — draw the progress tracker (below).
3. **Timer** — visible countdown on the projector, running for the whole block.
4. **Red slips** — a stack of red paper slips at the end of each row (or a red sticky-note pad). This is the "I'm stuck, come to me" signal — see §4.
5. **Know your problem set** — read all problems yourself before class. Know which one is hardest; that's usually (not always) the one you'll live-solve at the end.
6. **Know your duration** — decide the block length before you walk in (see §6 for how the script scales). Write the checkpoint minutes on a corner of the board so you don't have to do arithmetic live.

**Progress board:** one column per pod, one row per problem. Anchors add a tick when a member finishes. No names — counts only.

```
POD    A    B    C    D    E    F    G    H
P1     |||| |||  |||| |||| ||   |||| |||  ||||
P2     ||   |    |||  ||   |    |||  ||   |||
P3          |    |         |    |         |
MCQ    |||| |||  |||  |||| ||   |||| |||  ||||
```

## 2. Pods and anchors (set up once, keep all term)

**Groups of 7–8 students. A pod is a block of seats, not a group students choose.** If you sit there, you're in that pod. Nobody picks, nobody is left out, nobody moves.

Number of pods ≈ class size ÷ 7.

| Class size | Pods | Per pod |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 7 | 7–8 |
| 70 | 9 | 7–8 |
| 85 | 11 | 7–8 |
| 100 | 13 | 7–8 |

### 5-minute setup, once for the term

1. **Map the room before the block.** Sketch the seating, draw pod boundaries — usually 2 rows × 4 seats, or one row split in half. Equal blocks.
2. **Label physically.** Tape a sheet with the pod letter at the aisle end of each block. Leave it up all term.
3. **Announce it, 30 seconds:** *"Look at the letter nearest you. That's your pod for the rest of this course. You don't move, you don't swap. If you're sitting here, you're in that pod."*
4. **Anchors** — ask each pod for a volunteer. If nobody volunteers, **the student in the aisle seat is the anchor** — closest to the board, which is the job's only physical requirement. Rotate weekly by moving one seat along.
5. **Match the board to the room.** Board columns run left-to-right in the same order as the pods. Pod A is the far-left block *and* the far-left column.

### When things shift

- **Someone's absent** — pod is smaller today. Don't re-pod, don't merge.
- **Anchor's absent** — aisle seat takes over. That's why the rule is positional, not a named person.
- **Someone sits in the wrong block** — they're in that pod today. Not worth correcting.
- **New joiner** — they sit somewhere, that's their pod. No admin.

### The anchor's job — 3 things, none of them teaching

1. Mark ticks on the board when a pod member finishes.
2. Escalate a red slip to you when the pod can't resolve something in 2 minutes.
3. At the exit gate, confirm each pod member has one passing output.

Tell anchors once, every term: *"You are not a tutor. You are not expected to know the answers. You count, and you escalate. That's it."*

## 3. Never do these

- ❌ Stand silently at the front.
- ❌ Ask *"Any doubts?"* — nobody self-reports in a room of 70. Use the ladder count (§7) instead.
- ❌ Walk the aisles without stopping. Circulating without asking a scripted question at each desk teaches nobody anything and looks like supervision, not teaching.
- ❌ Take a student's keyboard and fix it yourself. Point at the line, ask the question, let them type.
- ❌ Let the block start by itself. You open every block from the front, scripted.
- ❌ Solve a problem on the board before students attempt it. The live solve (§10) always comes after the work stretches, never before.
- ❌ Skip a check because the room "looks busy." The check is what *makes* the room look busy — it's the pressure mechanism, not a progress report you can opt out of.
- ❌ Name a student who hasn't started, in public. The count is the pressure; naming costs you the room.

---

## 4. The red-slip signal

A student who is stuck for more than **2 minutes** puts a red slip on their screen — visibly, without raising a hand or turning around. You (or their anchor) come to them. This exists so:

- Students don't sit stuck in silence for 20 minutes out of embarrassment.
- You don't waste time scanning every screen for who needs help — the slip finds you.
- Struggle under 2 minutes is left alone on purpose — that's productive difficulty, not a problem to fix.

State the rule once, every block, in the norm-setting minute (§8): *"Stuck for more than two minutes — not ten — put a red slip on your screen. Don't raise your hand, don't turn around. Red slip."*

---

## 5. The core loop, at a glance

Every block, regardless of length, is built from the same seven pieces, run in this order:

1. **Launch** — get every screen on the same page, state the deal.
2. **Problem 1 together** — one problem, done as a class, so nobody starts cold.
3. **Norm-setting + Check** — state the red-slip rule, run the first ladder count.
4. **Work stretch(es)** — silent solo work, then pod talk allowed, you circulate with scripted questions. Broken into two routes (A then B) so you personally reach every part of the room.
5. **Periodic checks** — a 90-second ladder count, run 3 times total across the block, evenly spaced.
6. **Live solve** — you solve, live, the problem the room actually got stuck on.
7. **Exit gate** — one passing output shown per student, before anyone leaves.

Everything below is this loop with real minute numbers.

---

## 6. Scaling the script to any duration

The original script below is written for **60 minutes**. Most of it is fixed-length regardless of the total block — launch, the together-problem, each check, and the live solve don't get shorter just because the block is shorter. What flexes is **how much silent work time** you have, and that's split across two work stretches (Route A, Route B).

**Fixed-length pieces (33 min total, don't compress these):**

| Piece | Length |
|---|---|
| Launch | 5 min |
| Problem 1 together | 7 min |
| Norm-setting + Check 1 | 3 min |
| Check 2 | 3 min |
| Check 3 | 3 min |
| Live solve | 7 min |
| Exit gate | 5 min |

**Flexible piece:** `Work time = Total duration − 33 min`, split roughly 55% Route A / 45% Route B (Route A runs first and is slightly longer because it's also absorbing Check 1's tail).

| Total block | Work time | Route A | Route B | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45 min | 12 min | 7 min | 5 min | Tight. Consider dropping Problem-1-together to 5 min and cutting Check 3 (run only 2 checks) if the room is already warmed up from a prior block. |
| 60 min | 27 min | 15 min | 12 min | The reference script — see §8–§13 for full minute-by-minute. |
| 75 min | 42 min | 23 min | 19 min | Add a 4th check if you want (e.g. one more ladder mid-Route-A) but 3 is still enough. |
| 90 min | 57 min | 31 min | 26 min | Consider a 4th check (evenly spaced across the longer work time) and a second live-solve problem if two sticking points emerged. |

**Below ~35 minutes**, the fixed overhead eats too much of the block. In that case:
- Drop to **2 checks** instead of 3 (skip Check 2, or merge Check 2 and 3).
- Cut Problem-1-together to 5 min, or skip it entirely if this is a second practice block on the same problem set the same day (the room is already warmed up).
- Keep the live solve and exit gate — never cut those, they're where retention and accountability actually happen.

Write your own block's real clock times on the board before students arrive — don't do the subtraction live in front of the room.

## 7. The periodic check ("the ladder")

**Run this at roughly 25%, 55%, and 85% of the way through the block** — in the 60-minute reference script that's minutes 15, 30, and 45. It takes 90 seconds and it is the single biggest thing separating this from a silent room where you can't tell who's stuck.

Use a **ladder** — one pass gives you the whole distribution:

> *"Everyone stop. Hands up if you've finished at least one."*
> — count out loud —
> *"Keep them up if you've done two."*
> — count —
> *"Three."*
> — count —
> *"Four."*

Write the ladder in the corner of the board as you go:

```
15 min   ·  1+: 71   2+: 44   3+: 12
30 min   ·  1+: 78   2+: 61   3+: 38   4+: 15
45 min   ·  1+: 80   2+: 72   3+: 55   4+: 31
```

Then do two things, every time:

- **Compare to the last check, out loud.** *"Two-or-more went from forty-four to sixty-one in fifteen minutes. Keep going."*
- **Name the sticking point you saw while circulating** — the problem, not a student. *"About a third of you are stuck on the same thing in problem three. You're not alone — hold it, we solve it at the end."*

**Rules**
- **Never name a student who hasn't started.** The count is the pressure. Naming costs you the room.
- **Never skip a check because the room looks busy.** The check is what makes the room look busy.
- **If a number goes flat between checks**, that's your signal to change something *right now* — walk faster, call out a hint to the whole room, or shorten the next work stretch — not a thing to note and move past.

---

## 8. Launch (fixed, ~5 min)

Stand at the front. Do not start until the room is quiet.

> *"Laptops open. Go to the URL on the screen — right now, all of you. I'm going to look at the room in thirty seconds and I want to see the same page on every screen."*

Wait the thirty seconds. Scan the room. Say what you see:

> *"Row three, four of you are still on something else. I'll wait."*

Then state the deal — adjust the minute numbers to your actual block length:

> *"Here's the hour. In [Launch+P1] minutes every single person here will have finished problem one — I'm going to walk you into it. Then you work. At [live-solve start] minutes I solve the problem that most of you got stuck on, and if you haven't attempted it by then, watching me solve it will do nothing for you. Before you leave, you show me or your pod anchor one problem that runs. That's the deal."*

---

## 9. Problem 1 together (fixed, ~7 min)

Nobody works alone yet.

1. **Read problem 1 aloud.** The whole statement.
2. **Ask for the approach, not the code:** *"Don't give me syntax. In plain English, what does this program have to do?"* Take two answers.
3. **Everyone types it themselves.** Say the structure out loud. Do **not** type it on the projector. *"Type it. I'm not showing you — you have it."*
4. **Countdown:** *"Three, two, one — everyone hit run."*
5. **Hands up who got the right output.** Count out loud. *"That's fifty-one of you who just wrote a working program. Anchors, mark P1."*

**If a student's doesn't run** — do not fix it. Say *"read the error message and tell me the line number."*

---

## 10. Norm-setting + Check 1 (fixed, ~3 min)

> *"Next [work stretch 1 length] minutes: solo and silent. Timer's on the screen. If you're stuck for more than two minutes — two, not ten — put a red slip on your screen. Don't raise your hand, don't turn around. Red slip. I or your anchor will come to you."*

> *"And every [check interval] minutes I'm going to stop you and ask how many you've done. Hands, not names. Get used to it."*

Then run **Check 1** (§7) on problem 1.

---

## 11. Work stretches

Silent solo work. Timer visible. You circulate. Split the room into two halves so you personally reach every seat once per block.

**Route A** = the half of the room you normally reach last — back and far corners first. Runs during the first work stretch.
**Route B** = the half you didn't cover in Route A. Runs during the second work stretch, and — unlike Route A — pod talk is allowed here (see below).

**What to say at each desk — use these exactly, don't improvise a longer answer:**

| What you see | Say exactly this |
|---|---|
| Problem open, no code typed | *"Read me the first line you're going to write."* |
| Code typed, not run | *"Run it. Let's see what it says."* |
| Error on screen | *"Read the error out loud. What line?"* |
| Looks finished | *"Show me your output."* |
| Not on the platform at all | *"Which problem are you on?"* — flat, no scolding |

**Rules for yourself:**
- Never touch their keyboard. Point at the line, ask the question, let them type.
- 90 seconds max per student — you have too many desks to camp at one.
- **Note which problem people are stuck on.** You need this for the live solve.

**Second work stretch (Route B) only — pod talk allowed:**

> *"Next [work stretch 2 length] minutes you may talk to your pod. Quietly. Explaining it to someone else is how you find out whether you actually understand it."*

**Priority in Route B:** red slips first, then anyone whose screen hasn't changed since Route A.

**For a student stuck and frustrated** — hand them to a peer, don't rescue them yourself:
> *"Priya finished this one two minutes ago. Priya, walk him through your approach — don't give him the code."*

---

## 12. Live solve (fixed, ~7 min)

Solve the problem you actually saw people stuck on — not one you picked in advance. This is the payoff for having circulated and noted sticking points during the work stretches; if you skipped that, you're guessing here.

- Type it live, from scratch, talking through your thinking.
- **Make a deliberate mistake and read the error message aloud.** Students need to see that errors are normal, not a sign they did something uniquely wrong.
- Take the next step from the room: *"What goes on line two?"* — don't just narrate, pull answers out of students.

> *"If you attempted this before now, this is landing for you. If you didn't, this is the part you'll have to redo at home — and it'll take you three times as long alone."*

---

## 13. Exit gate (fixed, ~5 min)

> *"Before you leave: one problem that runs, shown to me or your pod anchor. Anchors, you're confirming your six."*

Stand at the door. Don't block anyone — just be visible.

Then:

> *"Last thing. In the chat — or out loud to your pod — say which problem you're doing first next session."*

Read the board on the way out — cite the real trend from your three checks:

> *"Board says fifty-two of you finished two or more today. Started at eleven three weeks ago. Same time tomorrow."*

---

## 14. Troubleshooting — situations the script doesn't cover by default

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Projector/platform down at launch** | Don't wait it out silently. Announce the delay, give students problem 1 to discuss with their pod on paper/verbally while you fix it. Never let the room sit idle without an instruction. |
| **Attendance far below normal** | Run pods as-is, smaller. Don't re-pod or merge — see §2. If under ~20 students total, you can skip pods entirely and run checks as a straight show-of-hands. |
| **Room is way ahead of the problem set (finishing early)** | Don't stretch the live solve to fill time. Pull in a bonus/stretch problem, or start the next session's problem 1 as a preview — announce this is what you're doing so it doesn't look like scope creep. |
| **Room is way behind (numbers flat at Check 2)** | Change something immediately, don't wait for Check 3: shorten Route B, give a hint to the whole room out loud, or move the live solve earlier and cut a work stretch short. |
| **One pod is clearly struggling as a group, not individuals** | That's an anchor-escalation pattern, not individual red slips. Spend a full circulate pass at that pod specifically during Route B. |
| **A student finishes everything early, repeatedly** | Give them a real extension (harder variant, or "explain your approach to two people who are stuck") — don't let them sit idle, and don't let "helping others" become unpaid TA work without you directing it. |
| **Hybrid/remote students in the mix** | Ladder counts need a parallel channel (poll/reaction) since you can't see hands. Red slips become a chat keyword (e.g. "STUCK"). Pods can still exist virtually but anchors need a way to see remote members' progress — a shared doc works. |
| **New instructor running this for the first time** | Run the script exactly as written for at least 2 blocks before adapting anything. The script's power is in students learning the ritual — changing it early confuses them more than it costs you in optimization. |
| **A student disputes their count / feels put on the spot by the ladder** | Hands, not names, always — that's the whole design. If someone still feels singled out, address it 1:1 after class, not by changing the public mechanic for everyone. |

## 15. One-page strip (60-min reference — scale per §6 for other durations)

| Time | Block | You are doing |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Launch** | Front of room, scripted start, scan screens, state the deal |
| 5–12 | **Problem 1 together** | Read aloud, take approach from room, countdown to run, count hands |
| 12–15 | **Set the norm + CHECK 1** | Solo work, timer, 2-min red-slip rule → **ladder count** |
| 15–30 | **Work stretch 1 (Route A)** | Back and corners first, scripted questions, 90 s cap |
| 30–33 | **CHECK 2** | **Ladder count**, compare to Check 1 out loud, name the sticking point |
| 33–45 | **Work stretch 2 (Route B)** | Pod talk allowed, red slips first, peer handoffs |
| 45–48 | **CHECK 3** | **Ladder count**, read the 15 → 30 → 45 trend off the board |
| 48–55 | **Live solve** | Type live, deliberate mistake, take next steps from the room |
| 55–60 | **Exit gate** | At the door, one passing output each, next-session commitment, read the board |

**Three checks, every block, no exceptions.** Roughly 25% · 55% · 85% of the way through, whatever the total length.
