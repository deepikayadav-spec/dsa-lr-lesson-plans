# Session 9 — Data Interpretation: Bar & Line Graphs

**Duration** 50 min total — **45 min instruction + 5 min buffer** (3 min settling at the start, 2 min flex at the end) · **Topic** Reading bar graphs and line graphs, and applying percentage/growth calculations to graphical data · **Prerequisite** DI Tables — the percentage-change formula and the four-step reading method
**Session type** Lecture + guided practice. No source slide deck — content from the session's concept-explanation text. No classroom quiz bank yet — 5-min slot reserved at end. the "Problem Solving" page is image-only and unrecoverable — all practice data below is instructor-authored. · **Format** 50-min recalibrated, 2 ALS activities

**Note on visuals:** the source's bar/line graph images are not recoverable. This plan represents the same graph data as tables (values that would sit on the axes) — when delivering live, sketch the bars/lines on the board from these values so students practice reading an actual visual, not just a table restated as a graph.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

1. Distinguish when a bar graph versus a line graph is the appropriate representation (categorical comparison vs. trend over time). *(UNDERSTANDING)*
2. Identify axes, scale, and legend on a graph and extract values accurately. *(APPLYING)*
3. Apply the four-step analysis method (identify graph type → understand relationships → calculate → focus on relevant data) to graph-based questions. *(APPLYING)*
4. Calculate year-on-year growth and identify the highest/lowest performer from multi-series graph data. *(ANALYZING)*

## Warm-Up Poll — Retrieval Practice on Session 8 (3–7 min) · ALS: Polling

Say: *"Five quick ones from last session."*

**Q1.** What's the formula for percentage change?
`A` (New − Old)/New × 100 · `B` (New − Old)/Old × 100 · `C` (Old − New)/Old × 100
→ *Read:* B is correct. If shaky, this session leans on it heavily — do a 30-second re-drill.

**Q2.** From last session's table: which region had the biggest raw sales numbers but actually declined?
`A` North · `B` South · `C` East · `D` West
→ *Read:* D is correct — confirms the "big numbers ≠ good performance" lesson stuck.

**Q3.** In a table/graph question, what should you do before calculating anything?
`A` Read every single number · `B` Understand what the question is actually asking · `C` Guess an estimate first
→ *Read:* B is correct — the four-step method's step 2.

**Q4.** Guess: a bar graph is usually best for comparing categories, while a line graph is usually best for showing what?
`A` Proportions of a whole · `B` Trends over time · `C` Exact single values only
→ *Read:* B is correct — seeds Teaching Block A directly.

**Q5.** How comfortable are you reading values off an axis (not a table) under time pressure?
`A` Very uncomfortable · `B` Okay with practice · `C` Comfortable
→ *Read:* If mostly A, slow down through the worked graph reads in Teaching Block A.

**Running it** — poll tool, ~45 s per question, ~3.75 min total.

## Hook (7–10 min)

Say: *"Stock market apps, weather apps, sales dashboards — almost none of them show you a raw table. They show you a graph, because a graph tells a story a table can't: is it going up, down, or staying flat, at a glance. Today you learn to read that story precisely, not just guess at the shape."*

Sketch a rough upward-trending line on the board with no numbers. Say: *"By the end of today, you'll be able to look at a line like this and tell me the exact percentage growth, not just 'it went up.'"*

## Teaching Block A (10–19 min) — TEACH FROM NOTES BELOW

Covers: bar vs. line graph purpose → axes/scale/legend → the four-step method → worked bar-graph reading.

**Beats to emphasise**

- **Bar graphs** compare quantities across categories (e.g. sales per region, this month). **Line graphs** show trends and changes over time (e.g. revenue across years). **Pie charts** (next session) show proportions of a whole.
- **Structural elements to check first, every time:** the axes (what does each one measure, and in what units), the scale (what does one gridline step represent — is it 10s, 100s, 1000s?), and the legend (which colour/pattern is which category, if more than one series is shown).
- **Four-step method, write on the board:**
  1. **Identify the type of graph** — bar, line, or pie — this tells you what kind of question it's built to answer.
  2. **Understand relationships between data points** — is one series above another, are they converging or diverging?
  3. **Perform the necessary calculation** — same formulas as tables (% change, average, comparison).
  4. **Focus on relevant data** — read the question first, don't extract every value on the graph.

**The worked data (instructor-authored, revenue by company, ₹ thousands, read as if off a multi-line graph):**

| Company | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 50 | 55 | 60.5 |
| B | 40 | 42 | 45 |
| C | 60 | 58 | 61 |
| D | 35 | 40 | 46 |
| E | 45 | 47 | 50 |

- **Worked example, live:** *"What was Company A's revenue growth from 2019 to 2020?"*
  - [(55 − 50)/50] × 100 = (5/50) × 100 = **10%**.
- **Say explicitly:** *"Notice this is the exact same formula from last session — the graph just changes how the numbers are presented, not the math itself."*

**Checkpoint (at 19 min)** — 10 s silent think, cold-call two students:
> *"Which company's line on this graph actually goes down before going back up?"*
> **Answer:** **Company C** — 60 → 58 (down) → 61 (back up).

## ⚡ ALS Activity 1 — Whiteboard Race: Read the Line (19–25 min)

**ALS format:** Paired Whiteboard Race — pairs race to compute year-on-year growth for an assigned company from the graph data, first correct board up wins the round. Chosen to build speed reading a multi-series graph before Teaching Block B's full-range comparison.

**Setup line:**
> *"Pairs, boards up, same data. I'll call a company and a year range — find the percentage growth. First correct board up wins. Three rounds."*

- Round 1: *"Company B, 2019 to 2020"* → (42−40)/40 × 100 = **5%**.
- Round 2: *"Company D, 2020 to 2021"* → (46−40)/40 × 100 = **15%**.
- Round 3: *"Company E, 2019 to 2021"* → (50−45)/45 × 100 = **11.11%**.

**How it surfaces:** After each round, ask the winning pair to state which year values they used before revealing the calculation — reinforces reading the correct two points off the "line" before computing.

**Debrief line:**
> *"Whether this data sits in a table or a graph, the read-then-calculate discipline is identical. The only new skill is finding the right point on an axis instead of a cell."*

**Cut rule:** If running short, cut to 2 rounds (drop Round 3), but keep the "state your two values first" requirement.

---

## Teaching Block B (25–32 min) — TEACH FROM NOTES BELOW

Covers: full-range comparison across companies, spotting the highest overall grower — a deliberate trap paralleling Session 8's regional trap.

**Beats to emphasise**

- **The trap:** *"Company D starts the lowest of all five (35) and ends solidly mid-table (46). Company C starts highest (60) and ends highest (61). Which company actually grew the most, in percentage terms, from 2019 to 2021?"* Don't answer yet — let students guess first, most will guess C because it has the biggest final number.
- **Worked full comparison, live:**
  - A: (60.5−50)/50 × 100 = **21%**
  - B: (45−40)/40 × 100 = **12.5%**
  - C: (61−60)/60 × 100 = **1.67%**
  - D: (46−35)/35 × 100 = **31.43%**
  - E: (50−45)/45 × 100 = **11.11%**
  - **Company D grew the most (31.43%)** — despite having the smallest final number on the graph.
- **Say explicitly:** *"The company with the biggest-looking bar or the highest line at the end isn't automatically the best performer. 'Biggest' and 'fastest-growing' are two completely different questions — always check which one you're actually being asked."*

**Checkpoint (at 32 min)** — cold-call:
> *"Which company grew the least, in percentage terms, from 2019 to 2021?"*
> **Answer:** **Company C**, at only 1.67% — despite having the highest absolute revenue every single year.

## ⚡ ALS Activity 2 — Silent Solve → Vote-Lock → Reveal: Rank Them All (32–40 min)

**ALS format:** Silent Solve, Vote-Lock, Then Reveal — students commit to a full ranking before the answer is revealed. Deliberately different register from Activity 1's loud paired race (quiet, individual, single big reveal), and directly tests whether the Teaching Block B trap actually landed.

**Setup line:**
> *"On your own, two minutes. Rank all five companies from highest to lowest percentage growth, 2019 to 2021. You already have the individual numbers from Teaching Block B if you were tracking — but work it out yourself. Write your ranking, hold it up when I say show."*

Give 2 minutes silent work (recalculation or recall), then: *"Show me — three, two, one, show."*

**The reveal, step by step (full ranking):**
1. **D — 31.43%**
2. **A — 21%**
3. **B — 12.5%**
4. **E — 11.11%**
5. **C — 1.67%**

> *"Notice the ranking by growth is almost the exact reverse of the ranking by final absolute revenue (C highest absolute, D lowest starting point but highest growth). That's not a coincidence in this data — it's a pattern worth watching for: strong absolute leaders often grow slower because they're already large."*

**Debrief line:**
> *"If a question asks 'which company is doing best,' always ask yourself — best by what measure? Size, or growth? They can point to completely different answers, like they do here."*

**Cut rule:** If running short, cut the full 5-way ranking to just "top 1 and bottom 1" but always reveal that D is the top grower and C is the bottom — that contrast is the entire point.

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Why students hold it | Correct it live by |
|---|---|---|
| The highest bar/line at the end = the best performer | Visual size dominates first impression | Teaching Block B + Activity 2's D-vs-C reveal |
| Bar graphs and line graphs need completely different math | Different visual appearance suggests different rules | Explicit "same formula, different presentation" framing throughout |
| A dip in a line graph (like Company C 2019→2020) means overall decline | Focusing on one segment instead of the full range | Checkpoint at 19 min, explicitly named and revisited |
| Growth ranking and size ranking are usually the same | Intuition that "bigger companies grow more" | Activity 2's explicit near-reverse-ranking observation |
| Reading a graph is fundamentally different from reading a table | Different visual format feels like a different skill entirely | Teaching Block A's explicit "exact same formula" framing |
