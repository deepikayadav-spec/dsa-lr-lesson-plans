# Introduction to Quantitative Aptitude

**Duration** 33 min total — 28 min instruction + 5 min Classroom Quiz · **Topic** Introduction to Quantitative Aptitude — what aptitude is, why it matters, course overview · **Prerequisite** None — day one
**Session type** Lecture · **Format** Condensed — active learning strategies referenced by name, single closing quiz

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

1. Define aptitude and state how it differs from general mathematics ability. *(REMEMBERING)*
2. Explain why aptitude is assessed so widely — recruitment drives, qualifying exams, government exams. *(UNDERSTANDING)*
3. Describe the structure of this Aptitude course — its four components. *(REMEMBERING)*
4. Apply an assume-and-adjust reasoning strategy to solve a classic heads-and-legs riddle. *(APPLYING)*

## Hook (4–7 min)

Say: *"Before I show you the next slide — guess. Out of the top IT recruiters in India, how many do you think run an aptitude round before they even look at your resume?"*

Take two or three shouted guesses. Then reveal: TCS, IBM, Cognizant, HCL, Accenture, Infosys, Deloitte, Wipro, Tech Mahindra, and more — 25 companies, one category of four.

> *"Every single one of them puts an aptitude round before the technical interview. Not after. Before."*

## Concept Walkthrough (7–16 min)

**Core idea:** aptitude tests how you reason and approach a problem under time pressure — not how fast you calculate. It's tested everywhere: recruitment drives (TCS, Infosys, Wipro...), qualifying exams (CAT, GATE, GMAT...), and government exams (UPSC, SSC, IBPS...). This course has four components — **Quantitative Aptitude, Logical Reasoning, Advanced Aptitude, Numerical Ability** — and everything from today onward lives inside the first of those four.

**Worked example — the goats-and-hens riddle:** *"A farmer has some goats and some hens. In total, there are 43 heads and 126 legs. How many goats?"* Assume, just for a moment, that all 43 are hens — that's 43 × 2 = 86 legs. But the real total is 126, a gap of 40. Each goat contributes 2 more legs than a hen would (4 − 2), so goats = 40 ÷ 2 = **20**. (Verify: 20 goats × 4 + 23 hens × 2 = 80 + 46 = 126. ✔)

**Checkpoint:**
> *"Name the four components of this Aptitude course."*
> **Answer:** Quantitative Aptitude, Logical Reasoning, Advanced Aptitude, Numerical Ability.

## ⚡ Active Learning Strategy 1 — Real-World Callout (16–21 min)

Students shout out any company, exam, or government test they personally know someone who's taken an aptitude round for, not restricted to the deck's lists — instructor sorts each callout into the right category live. Exposes that aptitude testing isn't an abstract syllabus topic but something students have already brushed up against.

---

## ⚡ Active Learning Strategy 2 — Silent Solve → Vote-Lock → Reveal (21–26 min)

Students work the goats-and-hens riddle alone or in pairs for two minutes and commit to an answer letter *before* any explanation, then the assume-all-hens technique is revealed step by step. Exposes whether students can commit to reasoning before seeing the trick — this is the session's one genuine problem-solving moment.

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Why students hold it | Correct it live by |
|---|---|---|
| "Aptitude is just another maths subject" | School experience only tested calculation, never reasoning-under-time separately | The walkthrough's aptitude-vs-maths distinction, reinforced by warm-up Q2 |
| "Aptitude only matters for IT company drives" | Recruitment drives are the most visible category | ALS 1's full spread across companies, exams, and government tests |
| "A riddle like this needs algebra to solve properly" | School conditioning to set up equations | ALS 2's reveal — assume-and-adjust, no equations |
| "This course is about calculating fast" | "Aptitude" sounds speed-focused | The four-component walkthrough — "methodologies & approaches," not just speed |
