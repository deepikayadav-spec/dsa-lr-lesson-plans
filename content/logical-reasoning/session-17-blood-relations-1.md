## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

1. Classify family members into generation layers (grandparents, parents/aunts/uncles, siblings/cousins, children). *(REMEMBERING)*
2. Represent a family structure using square (male) / circle (female) family-tree notation. *(APPLYING)*
3. Decode symbolic expressions (A+B=mother, A−B=brother, A×B=father, A÷B=sister) into plain relationships, including multi-step chains. *(APPLYING)*
4. Avoid common traps — gender assumptions, decoding a whole chain at once instead of step by step. *(EVALUATING)*

## Hook (7–10 min)

Say: *"A woman points to a man and says, 'He is the son of my father's wife.' Who is that man to the woman?"*

Give students 30 seconds, then solve together, step by step: *"My father's wife — for almost everyone, that's simply their mother. So the sentence becomes 'he is the son of my mother.' The son of your mother is... your brother."*

> *"That's the entire skill today — a sentence that sounds complicated is usually just one or two ordinary relationships stacked together. Once you decode each piece separately, the answer falls out on its own."*

## Teaching Block A (10–19 min) — TEACH FROM NOTES BELOW

<!-- placement: inferred grouping — no source deck; grouped from GitBook Concept Explanation extraction -->
Covers: generation classification → family tree notation → the four symbolic operators.

**Beats to emphasise**

- **Generation-based classification, write on the board:**
  - **1st generation:** Grandparents.
  - **2nd generation:** Parents, Uncles, Aunts.
  - **3rd generation:** Siblings, Cousins (your own generation).
  - **4th generation:** Children, Nieces, Nephews.
- **Family tree notation.** **□ (square) = Male**, **○ (circle) = Female**, connected with lines to show parent-child or marriage links. *"Sketching this takes ten seconds and prevents almost every gender mix-up."*
- **Symbolic operators — write all four on the board:**
  - **A + B** → A is the **mother** of B.
  - **A − B** → A is the **brother** of B.
  - **A × B** → A is the **father** of B.
  - **A ÷ B** → A is the **sister** of B.
- **Worked example, live (from the source, verified):** *"P × Q + R — decode it."*
  1. P × Q → **P is the father of Q.**
  2. Q + R → **Q is the mother of R.**
  3. Chain it: P is Q's father, Q is R's mother → **P is R's grandfather** (specifically, paternal grandfather, since the link runs through Q as R's mother, and P is Q's father).
- **Say explicitly:** *"Decode one symbol at a time, left to right. Never try to jump straight to the final answer — that's exactly where mistakes creep in."*

**Checkpoint (at 19 min)** — 10 s silent think, cold-call two students:
> *"M + N − O — decode it. What is M to O?"*
> **Answer:** M+N → M is mother of N. N−O → N is brother of O (so N and O are siblings, sharing the same mother) → **M is O's mother too.**

## ⚡ ALS Activity 1 — Whiteboard Race: Decode the Symbols (19–25 min)

**ALS format:** Paired Whiteboard Race — pairs race to decode a symbolic chain into a plain-English relationship, first correct board up wins the round. Chosen to drill the four operators into fast, automatic recall before Teaching Block B introduces longer, multi-generation chains.

**Setup line:**
> *"Pairs, boards up, operators visible on the board. I'll give you a symbolic chain — decode it into a plain relationship. First correct board up wins. Three rounds."*

- Round 1: *"A × B — what is A to B?"* → **Father.**
- Round 2: *"A ÷ B — what is A to B?"* → **Sister.**
- Round 3: *"X + Y × Z — what is X to Z?"* → X+Y → X is mother of Y. Y×Z → Y is father of Z. Chain: X is Y's mother, Y is Z's father → **X is Z's (paternal) grandmother.**

**How it surfaces:** After each round, have the winning pair narrate their decode step by step, out loud, before revealing the final relationship — the step-by-step habit is the actual skill, not just the final word.

**Debrief line:**
> *"Three rounds, and every single one came down to decoding one symbol, then the next, then combining. That's the whole method — there's no shortcut that skips the steps."*

**Cut rule:** If running short, cut to 2 rounds (drop Round 3), but always require the step-by-step narration.

## Teaching Block B (25–32 min) — TEACH FROM NOTES BELOW

Covers: the key solving strategy, and a worked complex chain building toward the Hook's answer format.

**Beats to emphasise**

- **Key strategy, write on the board:**
  1. **Sketch every relation** — even a quick square/circle diagram beats trying to hold it in your head.
  2. **Watch out for gender traps** — a name or pronoun doesn't always confirm gender the way you'd expect; rely on the stated relationship, not assumptions.
  3. **Decode step by step, not all at once** — exactly the discipline from Teaching Block A.
- **Worked example, live, a longer chain:** *"X ÷ Y × Z — decode it. What is X to Z?"*
  1. X ÷ Y → **X is the sister of Y.**
  2. Y × Z → **Y is the father of Z.**
  3. Chain it: X is Y's sister, Y is Z's father → **X is Z's (paternal) aunt.**
- **Revisit the Hook with the formal method:** *"He is the son of my father's wife"* — sketch it: draw the woman, her father (□), her father's wife (○, = the woman's mother in the standard reading), and "the son of" that circle. The son of the woman's mother, who isn't stated to be anyone else, is the woman's own brother — **confirmed: brother.**

**Checkpoint (at 32 min)** — cold-call:
> *"A × B ÷ C — decode it. What is A to C?"*
> **Answer:** A×B → A is father of B. B÷C → B is sister of C (so B and C are siblings, same father). → **A is C's father too.**

## ⚡ ALS Activity 2 — Silent Solve → Vote-Lock → Reveal: The Aunt Chain (32–40 min)

**ALS format:** Silent Solve, Vote-Lock, Then Reveal — students decode a fresh three-symbol chain on their own before the answer is revealed. Deliberately different register from Activity 1's loud paired race (quiet, individual, single big reveal), targeting a longer chain than any single round in Activity 1.

**Setup line:**
> *"On your own, ninety seconds. Decode: R + S ÷ T. What is R to T? Write your full step-by-step reasoning, not just the final word. Hold it up when I say show."*

Give 90 seconds silent work, then: *"Show me — three, two, one, show."*

**The reveal, step by step:**
1. R + S → **R is the mother of S.**
2. S ÷ T → **S is the sister of T** (so S and T are siblings, sharing the same mother).
3. Chain it: R is S's mother, S and T share the same mother → **R is T's mother too.**

**Debrief line:**
> *"Notice this one didn't need a new rule — a sibling symbol (÷ or −) almost always means 'this parent relationship applies to both of them.' That single insight solves more chains than any other trick today."*

**Cut rule:** If running short, skip the silent window and solve it together on the board — but always require the full step-by-step reasoning to be written, not just the final answer.

## Exit Ticket + Homework (45–48 min)

**Exit ticket** (~1 min):

> Decode: K − L + M. What is K to M?
> **Answer:** K−L → K is brother of L. L+M → L is mother of M. Chain: K is L's brother, L is M's mother → **K is M's (maternal) uncle.**

Scan responses on the way out — if the "sibling symbol carries the parent relationship across" insight isn't sticking, revisit briefly at the start of Session 18.

**Homework**

| Task | Note |
|---|---|
| Decode: A × B + C ÷ D — a four-symbol chain, find what A is to D | Self-check using the step-by-step method |

Tell them: *"Today was about symbols and structure. Session 18 flips the perspective entirely — riddles told in first person, 'I,' 'my,' 'myself,' where you have to build the family tree from the speaker's own point of view."*

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Why students hold it | Correct it live by |
|---|---|---|
| A whole symbolic chain can be decoded in one mental leap | Feels faster to skip steps once a few symbols are memorised | Teaching Block A's explicit "one symbol at a time" rule + Activity 1's required narration |
| A sibling symbol (−, ÷) only tells you about the two people directly named | Doesn't extend the shared-parent implication further down the chain | Checkpoint at 19 min + Activity 2's explicit "carries across" insight |
| Names or pronouns reliably indicate gender in these puzzles | Everyday assumption carried over from real life | Teaching Block B's explicit gender-trap warning |
| "Father's wife" always means someone other than "mother" | Overthinking a simple relationship because the question sounds formal | Hook's explicit resolution — in the standard reading, it IS the mother |
| Family tree sketching is optional if the chain seems short | Two- or three-symbol chains feel manageable without a diagram | Teaching Block B's explicit "sketch every relation" strategy, applied even to short chains |
